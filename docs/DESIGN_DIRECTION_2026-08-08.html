00

The problem isn't quality. It's sameness.

I read the codebase before writing a word of this. The build is genuinely top-decile: prerendered HTML so AI crawlers see real content, clean hydration, deferred WebGL that never taxes a mid-range Android, cookieless analytics, reduced-motion honoured in both canvases, no invented testimonials. I'm not here to re-audit that.

Here's what I see instead. Your visual language is #05060e ground, a four-stop cyan→indigo→violet→pink gradient, glass cards at 22px radius, radial glows, a floating orb, and a logo marquee. That is not a Revora identity. It is the house style of every AI-era startup site built since 2023. A Kolkata business owner comparing three dev shops on three browser tabs cannot tell you apart by looking — and looking is the first thing they do.

The sharper problem is that your design contradicts your copy. Read them side by side:

And there's a craft cost you're already paying. The gradient is on the title, the buttons, the scrollbar, the scroll-progress bar, the marquee bullets and the process numerals — and six per-service accent colours sit on top of it. That's ten colours competing for the same job. When everything glows, nothing is emphasised.

01

The idea: the document is the product.

Look at what a client actually receives from Revora. A fixed itemised quote. A written one-page roadmap. Milestone invoices. Weekly demo notes. At handover: full source, documentation, credentials, deployment access. Every single one of those is a document they keep — and keeping it is the point, because zero lock-in means the paperwork is the guarantee.

Nobody else in this market sells that. Freelancers don't produce it; agencies bury it behind account managers. It's your actual differentiator and your site currently expresses it in body copy while the design shouts something else entirely.

So build the site out of that world. Not glass and glow — ruled ledger paper, hanging margin rules, mono figures, stamps, signed commitments, tabular columns. The bahi khata and the engineering spec sheet, which every Indian business owner already reads as this is a serious firm that keeps records.

This document you're reading is built in that language. It's the pitch and the specimen at the same time.

The move that follows from it: your site's central act should be handing the visitor a document, not promising one. You already promise a written roadmap within 48 hours. Generate a real one on screen in 40 seconds instead. That's §03, and it's the highest-leverage change in this proposal.

Motion doctrine

Three rules
  1. Motion must carry meaning. Cut the blanket fade-up-on-scroll and the hover-lift on every card. Keep motion for the roadmap assembling itself, numbers counting to a real figure, and the service diagrams. Ambient motion everywhere is the loudest AI-generated tell there is.
  2. One stage per page. A single dark WebGL panel inset into the paper, framed like an instrument screen.
  3. Transitions belong to navigation. You already ship cross-document view transitions. Spend them on the service tile morphing into its service page — the one place a transition tells the visitor where they went.

On the 3D you already built

Don't delete it. Give it a job.

The hero orb is decoration — it depicts nothing. The six showcase scenes are the opposite: they're genuine system diagrams in motion, and they're the best asset on the site. So retire the orb and promote the scenes. One dark stage, inset into paper, showing the diagram for whichever service the visitor is reading. A dark instrument panel on a light document reads as a screen; dark-on-dark reads as wallpaper.

There's a code dividend too. Your stylesheet currently re-declares the entire token set four times to force “dark stage” areas inside light mode — .hero, .showcase-canvas, the navbar, the preloader. Paper-first makes that distinction real instead of a fight, and those overrides delete themselves.

03

The instrument: generate the roadmap on the page

Your strongest offer is buried in form microcopy: “describe your problem in a few lines and within 48 hours you get a one-page roadmap — recommended system, phases, timeline and a ballpark range.” That is a genuinely good offer sitting behind a form, a wait, and an act of trust from someone who has never heard of you.

Collapse it. Two questions, one button, and the visitor is holding the document. It's deterministic mapping — no model call needed for v1 — and it does four jobs at once: proves you understand their problem, qualifies the lead by service and scale, gives them something to forward to their business partner, and demonstrates the exact product thinking you sell.

Try it. This is working, not a mockup of one.

    05

    Designing for the readers who aren't human

    A growing share of your buyers will never see the site. They'll ask an assistant “who builds custom CRMs in Kolkata?” and read the answer. You've already done the hard part — prerendering means ChatGPT, Claude and Perplexity see full content rather than an empty <div id="root">. Four cheap additions finish the job:

    • /llms.txt — a plain-text brief: who you are, the six services, where you are, price bands, how to contact. It's becoming the robots.txt of answer engines and it costs an afternoon.
    • Machine-readable services — emit services.json from the same content.js that renders the page, so there's one source of truth and it can't drift.
    • Per-service OG cards — generate the six social images at build time from the design system instead of shipping one og-image.png for every URL.
    • Roadmap outputs as real pages — each generated roadmap gets a shareable URL. The prospect forwards it to their partner, and that link is a referral you didn't have to ask for.

    07

    Three decisions only you can make

    Everything above is mine to build. These three aren't, and two of them have been open across all three review rounds.

    • Your real price bands

      A floor for a pilot, a range for a build, a range for a platform. Wide is fine. Without them the generator hands over a document with its most important line blank, and the FAQ keeps saying “it depends.” This blocks Phases 2 and 3.

    • Paper-first, or dark-first?

      I'm recommending paper as the default with one dark stage, because it's the sharpest break from your competitors, it fits the document thesis, and it deletes four sets of token overrides. It's also the biggest single change to how the site feels, so it's your call, not mine. Say the word and I'll build the same system dark-first.

    • What the guarantee card commits you to

      Signed and dated makes those four promises feel binding — which is the point, and the reason to check them first. 24-hour response and a 7-day first demo are operational commitments for two people. Keep them only if you'll honour them on a bad week.
